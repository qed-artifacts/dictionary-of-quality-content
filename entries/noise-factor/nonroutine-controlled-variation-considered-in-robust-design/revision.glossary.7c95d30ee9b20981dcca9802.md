# Noise factor

*Scope: nonroutine-controlled variation considered in robust design*

In robust design, a noise factor is an input or operating condition whose variation affects a product or process response but is not routinely held at one fixed value.

Examples include ambient conditions, material differences, and wear during use. Designers may vary such factors deliberately in experiments to find settings that keep performance stable.

Noise here means an influence that can disturb performance, not necessarily sound or random measurement error.
