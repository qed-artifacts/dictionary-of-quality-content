# Ceiling effect

*Scope: upper-limit compression in a score scale*

A ceiling effect occurs when a measure's upper limit prevents it from distinguishing higher levels of the attribute it is meant to assess.

When many people score at or near the maximum, genuine differences or later improvement may be hidden.

The effect depends on the instrument and population; a high score alone does not establish how far beyond the scale's limit someone might be.
